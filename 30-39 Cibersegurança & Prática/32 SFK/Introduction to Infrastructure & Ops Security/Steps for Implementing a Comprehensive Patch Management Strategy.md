1. **Scanning (Detect Missing Patches)**  
    Perform scans to detect missing patches in your software and hardware components. These scans help identify vulnerabilities that could potentially be exploited.
2. **Prioritization**  
    Not every patch demands immediate action. Analyze and prioritize them based on the risk and potential impact associated with the vulnerabilities they are designed to address.
3. **Test Patches**  
    Prior to rolling out any patch, conduct tests in a controlled environment. This helps ensure that the patch will not conflict with existing systems or introduce new issues.
4. **Deployment**  
    Once the patch has been tested, proceed with the deployment phase. This may entail temporary system downtime, so the deployment should be strategically scheduled to minimize operational interruptions.
5. **Verification**  
    After the patches are deployed, it’s essential to confirm that they were correctly installed and that they have not caused any adverse effects within the system.
6. **Documentation**  
    Keep thorough records detailing the patches applied. This should include the reason for the patch, which assets were affected, and any complications or additional steps taken during the process.

A case in point highlighting the criticality of patch management is the [Equifax breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Equifax_data_breach), which occurred due to a lapse in updating a known vulnerability. Regular updates, coupled with a well-devised patch management strategy, are key to averting such security incidents.

Keeping your systems updated is a fundamental yet often overlooked aspect of infrastructure security. The use of outdated software can expose your systems to a multitude of security vulnerabilities.

Updates often contain patches for known vulnerabilities that hackers could exploit. Therefore, a robust patch management process is crucial in maintaining the security of your infrastructure. It involves acquiring, testing, and installing multiple patches (code changes) to an administered computer system.
